What happens as a result

What we see from inside the Core Hospitals: our observation, not yet a quantified study.
✕
Small, easy-to-start trials. Eight in three years is a hard number, and a small trial counts the same as a large one.
✕
More papers. Forty-five is a hard number too.
✕
Support is penalized. Every statistician, data manager and dollar moved to another institution's study is not spent on the hospital's own count.
The Ministry knows this. Its review this year keeps support as a floor, because the effort behind support varies too much to be scored.
That is exactly the problem your funders have had to solve.

Three shifts we propose

Re-weight counting, do not abolish it. Each shift has a working counterpart in how NIH funds research infrastructure.
1Input → outcome
Count trials that finish and report. Judge approvals and guideline changes case by case, over a longer period.
2In-house → support
Credit research the hub enabled at other institutions as the hub's own achievement.
3Single site → network
Cross-cutting infrastructure at the core; disease-specific networks carry the rest.
NIH review, in the funding announcements we read (CTSA hubs, TIN, coordinating centers of Duke, the CCTSI and Vanderbilt): no in-house trial counts, no first-author papers, no points or weights. Reviewers judge coordination, data and statistics, consortium contribution and dissemination.
